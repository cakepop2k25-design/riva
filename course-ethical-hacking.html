COURSE 04 · BEGINNER

Ethical Hacking Introduction

Learn the mindset, permission requirements, and reporting habits behind responsible security testing.

Lesson 1 — Permission comes first

Ethical testing requires clear authorization from the system owner. Permission should identify the systems in scope, allowed methods, timing, and who to contact if something goes wrong.

Lesson 2 — Scope and rules of engagement

Read the rules before starting. Stay within the approved assets and techniques. Stop if you encounter sensitive data or an unexpected impact, and follow the owner's reporting process.

Lesson 3 — Reconnaissance fundamentals

Reconnaissance is collecting information relevant to an authorized assessment. Start with documentation and public information about your own lab, and avoid collecting private information about people.

Lesson 4 — Practice in a legal lab

Use intentionally vulnerable training environments, local virtual machines, or learning platforms that explicitly authorize testing. Keep lab systems isolated and avoid testing public systems without permission.

Good practice environment: PortSwigger Web Security Academy offers free web security lessons and labs designed for learning.

Lesson 5 — Write a useful report

A good report explains the issue, impact, affected in-scope asset, safe reproduction details, evidence that avoids exposing secrets, and a practical fix. Be clear and respectful.

Knowledge check

1. What must you have before testing a system?

2. What should you do if testing reveals unexpected sensitive data?

3. What makes a useful security report?
